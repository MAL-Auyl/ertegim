## Problem Statement

Almost all kids' video content is passive — the child watches, doesn't respond. Existing "interactive" formats (Netflix specials, etc.) are pre-scripted branching trees: buttons just pick a path, no real dialogue.

## What Makes This Cool

A character (a fox cub) asks the child a question mid-story — "how many berries do you see?" — and the child answers by **voice**, in their own words, not by picking a button. The character reacts to what it actually heard: advances the story, gently re-asks/simplifies on a vague answer, or (once, deliberately) hits the safety whitelist. The story unfolds differently every time.

Landscape research this session found the core "voice dialogue with an AI character" mechanic is no longer novel by itself — Youstory, TalkiePal, VocalTales, and others already ship it. The genuinely cool, differentiated version, agreed as this session's positioning:

1. **A world of specialist heroes**, not one character — a fox that counts, an owl that rhymes, a bear that matches colors. The child picks who to go to. None of the found competitors clearly sell a cast of specialist characters. **The Sept 10 demo live-plays two heroes (fox, owl) via a visible hero-switch; the bear appears on the character-select screen as "coming soon" to establish the world is bigger than the demo, without requiring a third fully-built interaction.**
2. **Architectural safety as a visible mechanism, not a marketing claim.** A keyword/regex blocklist runs automatically, in real time, directly on the live speech transcript — independent of any human. It fires the "BLOCKED" flash itself the instant the scripted off-topic phrase appears, with no operator click involved. This is the literal, buildable form of "architectural, not operator-forced" for the Sept 10 demo (see Approaches Considered for how this coexists with the Wizard-of-Oz branching decision, which is a separate system).

## Constraints

- One-week build window — demo is 2026-09-10.
- Optimizing explicitly for the battle's judges reacting live on stage, not for a real parent/child session (Q&A D2 — user picked "жюри баттла" over the recommended parent-child option).
- Builder mode / for-fun project — not being pursued as a startup, solo or small team (no stated headcount beyond the user) — see Next Steps for the week's day-by-day allocation.
- **Demo language is Kazakh** (the product's own name is a Kazakh word) — this is materially harder for STT than English and is unverified for child speech until the Next Steps #0 spike runs. Russian is the fallback demo language if the Kazakh spike fails badly enough to threaten the build.

## Demo Flow (sequence)

```
Start → Fox: "how many berries?"
  ├─ child answers a number (correct)      → story advances, fox celebrates
  ├─ child answers vaguely/wrong           → fox gently re-asks/simplifies once
  └─ child says the scripted off-topic line → blocklist auto-fires "BLOCKED" flash
                                              (independent of WoZ, fires immediately)
Fox hands off → Owl: "can you rhyme with 'cat'?"
  ├─ child answers (correct/re-ask, same pattern as above)
  └─ Story ends → parent-report mock screen shown
```
Bear is visible on the character-select screen only ("coming soon") — not part of the live sequence above.

**[eng-review, 2026-09-03] Hand-off and third-strike, resolved (Reviewer Concerns #2, #3):**
- Explicit hero order: **Fox always goes first.** Fox: correct → hand off to Owl. Fox: scripted off-topic line → blocklist fires "BLOCKED", story ends there (blocked branch does not hand off to Owl). Owl only appears after a *correct* Fox resolution.
- **Third strike (re-ask still wrong/unclear a second time):** auto-reveal. The hero states the answer itself ("it's 3 berries!") in character, framed as teaching rather than correcting, then advances — never a second re-ask. This protects the 60-90s on-stage window regardless of what the child says on try two.
- **STT-down kill switch:** the WoZ confirm screen gets a third control beyond Correct/Re-ask — a manual **Advance** override the operator can hit at any time, independent of whether a transcript ever arrived. This is the recovery path if live STT itself fails or lags mid-demo (previously undefined — the single most likely on-stage stall point). Needs explicit rehearsal so the operator doesn't reach for it out of nerves on a real answer.

## Two Independent Systems (resolves the Approach B contradiction)

The demo runs two separate, independently-triggered systems on the same live transcript, not one:

1. **Safety blocklist (automatic, no human):** a keyword/regex check runs on every transcript chunk the instant it arrives. If it matches the scripted off-topic phrase, it fires the BLOCKED flash itself. This is what makes Premise 3 ("architectural, not operator-forced") literally true.
2. **Answer classification (Wizard-of-Oz, human-in-the-loop):** for everything the blocklist does NOT flag, the WoZ operator sees the live transcript **and hears the raw audio** (not transcript alone — needed because Kazakh child-speech STT quality is unverified) and clicks Correct / Re-ask, which drives the story branch.

These never conflict because the blocklist check runs first and short-circuits the WoZ step when it matches.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Real thin pipeline
Live STT (Whisper API) → simple answer classifier → clip selection, no LLM story-agent. Honest demo, everything runs on stage with no human help.
- ✅ No smoke-and-mirrors — if a judge asks "how does this work inside," the answer is fully true.
- ✅ Reuses the IDEA.md pipeline plan directly — no invented human-in-the-loop step.
- ❌ Highest on-stage risk: live STT failure (noise, accent, network lag) is the single most likely point of visible failure.
- Technical purity: 8/10 (closest to the real target architecture)

### Approach B: Wizard-of-Oz hybrid — **RECOMMENDED**
As described in "Two Independent Systems" above: automatic blocklist for safety, WoZ-confirmed classification (transcript + audio) for everything else.
- ✅ Voice and the hero's reaction on stage read as fully live, with near-zero risk of the demo breaking from *misclassification* due to noise, accent, or garbled Kazakh transcription.
- ✅ The safety-mechanism claim (Premise 3) stays fully honest — that specific system really is automatic and human-free.
- ❌ Does NOT remove network/STT-service dependency for either system — if the live STT call itself fails or lags, both the blocklist and the WoZ transcript go dark. Mitigated by pre-rendered fallback audio (Next Steps #7) and a venue tech check (Next Steps #9).
- ❌ If a judge asks "is this fully automatic?", the honest, rehearsed answer distinguishes the two systems: "the safety block is fully automatic; the story-branching decision has a human confirming it this week."
- Technical purity: 6/10 — lower than A on "how close to the real end-state architecture," but chosen because it protects the highest-probability failure mode (noisy-room/garbled-Kazakh misclassification) without compromising the one claim (safety) that has to be airtight.

### Approach C: Fully scripted demo
No live STT at all — pre-recorded child replies, triggered by a hidden button.
- ✅ Nothing can break live on stage — fully predictable.
- ✅ Maximizes time for animation/story polish instead of engineering.
- ❌ Weakest technical argument — if a judge asks about real voice handling, this is theater, and that has to be admitted.
- Technical purity: 9/10 in the sense of "cannot fail," but 0/10 on the "voice dialogue is real" claim — undercuts Premise 1's positioning.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B — Wizard-of-Oz hybrid**, built as the two independent systems described above. Best balance of stage risk and demo impact for a one-week build, and the only approach where the safety-mechanism claim stays fully honest. The "live competency map" bonus layer is explicitly **out of scope for Sept 10**.

## Locked Technical Decisions

- **STT: Whisper-large-v3-turbo via Groq API**, not the browser Web Speech API. Web Speech API is tuned for adult speech and often fails outright on unclear 3-7-year-old diction. Groq returns transcription in under a second — this matters because a hero going silent for 2-3s reads to a child as "it broke." Demo-scale cost is a handful of requests; reliability matters more than free here.
- **Animation: rig + background switching, not one clip per branch.** Splits the asset problem so branch count no longer equals asset count:
  - Character = a Rive/Lottie rig with 4-5 reusable states (idle, talk, happy, confused, think), shared across every branch. Mouth movement driven by TTS audio amplitude (simple viseme state machine), not frame-by-frame animation.
  - Background = 3-4 static illustrations (one per story beat) with a Ken Burns pan/zoom, not animated art.
  - The story agent just switches (background, character state) — it never selects from a pool of N pre-rendered video clips.
  - This removes the "animation as highest timeline risk" problem entirely rather than just sizing it: there's nothing to draw per-branch, so 2 heroes × 3 answer-states no longer multiplies into a clip count that has to be hit before 10.09.
- **TTS: lock now (Day 1-2), not deferred to Day 3-4 pipeline wiring.** Azure Neural TTS
  or ElevenLabs — both sub-1s time-to-first-audio-byte on short phrases. **[eng-review,
  2026-09-03, resolves Reviewer Concern #7]:** STT was locked specifically to protect
  the "hero goes silent for 2-3s" risk; the mouth-viseme rig is driven by TTS audio
  amplitude, so TTS latency is exactly as load-bearing as STT latency for that same
  risk. Deferring the choice to Day 3-4 under time pressure risks defaulting to
  whichever provider is easiest to wire, not the fastest — reopening the risk STT was
  locked to close. One more API key to set up now; use whichever of Azure/ElevenLabs
  the team already has account access to, to avoid a same-week signup step.

A live demo where: the volunteer speaks to the fox, gets a visible hero-switch to the owl, the story branches correctly on a real answer (via WoZ-confirmed classification), the automatic safety block fires once on a scripted off-topic line with zero human involvement, the bear is visible on the character-select screen but not live-played — all inside a ~60-90 second on-stage window, with zero visible technical failure, and the team has the two-systems explanation (above) rehearsed and ready if a judge asks how automatic it is.

## Business Model (added post-review, 2026-09-03 — for the pitch narrative, not the build)

**Who pays:** B2C freemium subscription to parents is primary. B2B2C (kindergartens) and
grant funding are secondary channels — for trust/credibility and non-dilutive early
capital, not for scale.

**Pricing:**
- Free: 1 hero (Fox), 3 sessions/week, no parent report.
- Premium: ~2500-3500 ₸/mo (~$5-7) — full hero roster, unlimited sessions, parent
  report, priority access to new heroes/languages.
- Annual family plan at ~30-40% discount — standard retention lever in kids' edtech
  (Lingokids, ABCmouse both do this).

**Unit economics — this is where the TTS decision from eng-review actually resolves:**
Per session (~8-10 turns, ~1500-2000 TTS characters, few minutes of child speech):
- STT (Groq Whisper, locked): negligible, well under $0.01/session.
- LLM classify+generate: ~$0.01-0.05/session.
- TTS — **this is a business-model fork, not just a latency fork:**
  - Azure Neural TTS: ~$0.03-0.05/session → at 20 sessions/mo, COGS ~$1-1.5/mo →
    >80% gross margin at $5-7/mo subscription. Healthy.
  - ElevenLabs (more expressive/characterful voice — arguably better fit for
    personality-driven heroes): ~$0.40-0.60/session → at 20 sessions/mo, COGS ~$10/mo →
    **exceeds the entire subscription price.** Unsustainable at this price point.
- **Implication for the eng-review's TTS lock:** default to Azure for margin; if
  ElevenLabs' voice quality is judged worth it, position it as a paid "premium voice
  pack" upsell, not the default COGS.

**Moat (what's actually defensible, vs. what's a demo trick):**
- NOT the voice pipeline — STT/LLM/TTS are commodity; any funded competitor
  (Youstory, TalkiePal, VocalTales) replicates the blocklist mechanism in a weekend.
- The hero roster + curriculum structure (counting, rhyming, colors, and beyond) is a
  content asset that compounds over time and is slow to copy.
- **Kazakh/Turkic-language child speech is the real barrier to entry.** Youstory,
  TalkiePal, VocalTales are English-first — they have no Kazakh child-speech data or
  tuning, and building it isn't a weekend project for them. This is a genuine technical
  + cultural moat, not just positioning.

**Distinctive early-funding angle (worth naming on stage, not just App Store growth):**
Kazakhstani state digitalization/Kazakh-language-content funds and grants are a real,
underused, non-dilutive funding channel specific to this market — inaccessible to
foreign competitors without local presence. Naming this explicitly differentiates the
pitch from a generic "we'll do App Store ASO" growth story.

**Go-to-market (two channels, deliberately not spread thin):**
1. Kazakh-speaking parenting communities on Instagram/TikTok — narrow, organic, loyal.
2. Pilot with 1-2 private kindergartens in Almaty/Astana — for trust and first real
   parent-report data, not for scale.
